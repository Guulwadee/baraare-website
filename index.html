baraare /ba·raa·re/ Somali · noun prosperity; abundance.

Prosperity you can count.

Baraare helps Somali farmers and herders earn more from their land and animals by teaching them to track their costs and profits, know fair market prices, access water, and work together in cooperatives.

The problem we solve

Skilled producers, missing numbers.

Somali farmers and herders are experts at production. What they lack is economic information about their own enterprises. That one gap has three compounding costs.

  1. 01

    Invisible losses

    Without cost tracking, an unprofitable practice can persist for years. Nobody sees the loss, so nobody stops it.

  2. 02

    Weak bargaining

    The buyer knows the market price. The seller doesn’t. The gap between them is captured by intermediaries.

  3. 03

    No access to credit

    With no records, a producer is unbankable — no matter how well the farm or herd actually performs.

These are information problems before they are capital problems.

So that is where Baraare starts. We are a capability organisation, not a distribution one: we teach skills that stay.

Our approach

Six steps, one ladder.

Every program stands on the record-keeping habit built before it. We climb in order — and we don’t skip rungs.

  1. 01

    Records

    Pocket ledgers. Costs and profits, written down.

    Phase 1 · 2027
  2. 02

    Prices

    Fair market prices by print, SMS and WhatsApp.

    Phase 1 · 2027
  3. 03

    Cooperatives

    Group savings and loan-ready files.

    Phase 1 · 2027
  4. 04

    Water

    Committees, then rehabilitated water points.

    Phase 2 · 2027–29
  5. 05

    Breeds

    Improved breeding stock.

    Phase 3 · 2029+
  6. 06

    Breeding services

    Artificial insemination with licensed vets.

    Phase 3 · 2029+

Who we prioritise

Women and youth first.

  • Women

    A minimum of half of every training cohort, by design — not by exception.

  • Youth, 18–35

    Recruited and paid as facilitators, not just counted as participants.

  • Smallholder farmers

    Family farms that sell small surpluses and deserve to know their margins.

  • Pastoralist households

    Herding families managing livestock across the rangelands.
