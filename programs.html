Programs

The six-step ladder.

Records first. Then prices, then cooperatives — and only then water, breeds and breeding services. Each rung makes the next one work, so we climb in order and we don’t skip.

Phase 1 · Pilot · 2027

Records, prices, cooperatives.

The pilot teaches the three capabilities everything else stands on. Our first cohort will train 120+ producers, at least half of them women — a target we publish now and report against later.

1. Record-keeping & cost–profit literacy

Paper first. Every participant receives a pocket ledger and learns to write down what each animal or plot costs and what it earns — feed, water, transport, sale price, gross margin. Simple columns, filled in weekly.

We return at week 4 and week 12 to strengthen the habit, because the habit — not the training day — is the product.

2. Fair market-price information

We compile local market prices from public sources only and pass them on as printed sheets, SMS and WhatsApp messages. A seller who knows the going rate negotiates from level ground.

Every sheet is labelled as Baraare reference information. We are not a statistics office and we do not publish official figures.

3. Cooperative formation & bankability

We help producers organise: a constitution, a member register, group savings, minutes a bank can read. Together these become a loan-readiness file — evidence that this group keeps records and keeps its word.

A cooperative that can show its numbers stops being invisible to lenders.

Phase 2 · Water · 2027–2029

Water, in order.

Water is our major second pillar, and it follows the same logic as the ledger: information first, structures second, works third.

  1. Map — water-point mapping: location, condition, ownership, seasonality.
  2. Organise — Water User Committees with signed, written rules.
  3. Rehabilitate — boreholes restored by licensed contractors, with Baraare as a consortium sub-grantee. New boreholes come later.

Three conditions are non-negotiable before any works: a signed community water agreement, a professional hydrogeological study, and government permits. We do not drill; licensed contractors do.

Phase 3 · Livestock breeding · 2029+

Better animals, done properly.

Once records, prices and water are working, we turn to genetics: improved breeding stock first, artificial insemination services after — delivered only through licensed veterinary partners.

A herder with a ledger knows exactly what a better animal is worth. That is why breeding sits at the top of the ladder, not the bottom.

What we hold ourselves to.
